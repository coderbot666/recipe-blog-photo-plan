# Planning a Recipe Blog Photo Set Without a Studio

Food bloggers usually shoot after cooking, in whatever light is left. A small plan makes the difference between one usable photo and a full post.

## 1. Decide the three shots before you cook
Every recipe post needs the same three images: the finished dish from 45 degrees, a close detail (crumb, sauce, steam), and one "in progress" shot of hands or ingredients. Write them on a sticky note so you do not forget the middle step once the pan is hot.

## 2. Use one window and one board
Place the plate near a side window, turn off ceiling lights, and put a white board or cardboard on the opposite side to lift the shadows. Mixed light sources are what make food look grey.

## 3. Keep props boring
A plain plate, one napkin, one utensil. Busy patterns compete with the food and look dated within a year.

## 4. Make the cover image separately
The Pinterest or blog header often needs a vertical layout with space for a title. Instead of reshooting, you can extend the background or add a clean title area with an AI image editor. I use [Seedream 5.5](https://seedream5-5.net/) for this: describe the change ("extend the wooden table upward, leave space for a title") and keep the real dish untouched. Readers should always see the actual food you made.

## 5. Check on a phone
Most readers will see the image at phone size. If the dish is not recognisable as a thumbnail, crop tighter.

A repeatable plan beats expensive gear. Shoot the same three angles every time and the blog starts to look consistent on its own.
